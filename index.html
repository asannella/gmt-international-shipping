GMT Games · International selling · Draft for discussion

Getting GMT's games to players worldwide, without the customs surprises

GMT already ships to every country it legally can from Hanford. The hard part is everything that happens after the box leaves the building: VAT nobody can calculate, duty bills that land on the player's doorstep, shipping costs that don't reflect the real box, and stock that lives in more than one country. This page lays out what we're solving, where GMT is today, and how each piece gets handled on Shopify.

What the solution needs to do

G1Get compliant

Stop estimating international VAT, GST and import duties. Calculate them correctly on every order.

G2No surprise customs bills

Collect duties and taxes at checkout and show them clearly, so players never pay again on delivery.

G3Lighten the compliance load

Reduce or hand off the work of registering, filing and remitting taxes in other countries.

G4Better international rates

Lower, accurate shipping costs out of the US, priced on the real box size and weight.

G5One view of every location

See and route inventory across Hanford, the Australian distributor, stock coming from China, and future 3PLs.

G6P500 works worldwide

International players can pre-order and get charged later, just like US players.

Two ways to handle compliance

Everything else on this page works the same either way. The real choice is who acts as the legal seller on international orders.

Current direction

GMT stays the seller, with the right tools

Shopify Markets for localized selling, a duty and tax engine (Zonos) to calculate and guarantee landed cost, and DDP shipping labels so duties are prepaid. GMT keeps control of checkout, P500 and pricing.

Worth evaluating

A merchant of record takes over

A provider such as Global-e or ESW becomes the legal seller on international orders and takes on tax registration and remittance. It hands off the most work, but needs confirming against P500, multiple ship-from locations, and pricing.

Why not Shopify Managed Markets? It covers consumer orders shipped from the continental US on Managed Markets labels (Shopify Help Center). P500's charge-later model and GMT's non-US locations are a poor fit, so it's set aside for now.

How each goal gets solved

G1Get compliant on VAT, GST and duties

  • Every product gets an HS code and country of origin. Shopify uses these to calculate duties and import taxes at checkout (Help Center).
  • Zonos Duty and Tax adds a landed-cost engine. Per its listing, Zonos pays any difference on the customs bill, so GMT only pays what it collected (Shopify App Store).
On the diagram: replaces the guesswork on the Hanford to international and EU lanes.

G2No surprise customs bills

  • Duties and taxes are collected at checkout. Each market can show them as a separate line item or include them in the price (Help Center).
  • Orders ship on DDP labels, so the duty is already paid when the box arrives. Shopify requires a DDP-capable carrier when it collects duties at checkout.
On the diagram: the "International players" box now shows duties at checkout.

G3Lighten the compliance load

  • If GMT stays the seller: Zonos' listing says it remits VAT for you, with no need to register for IOSS or get a UK VAT ID. GMT still owns compliance in other countries.
  • If a merchant of record takes over: the provider becomes the legal seller and handles registration and remittance where it operates.
On the diagram: the duty and tax engine box. This is where the two paths differ.

G4Better, accurate international rates

  • Shopify Shipping offers discounted labels from USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL Express and DHL eCommerce for US merchants (Help Center).
  • Through ShipStation, GMT can add consolidators such as Asendia, Landmark Global or ePost Global, which pool parcels into the EU and UK and offer DDP services. Rates are quoted per merchant, so we'd get quotes on GMT's actual box sizes.
  • Saved package sizes give rates priced on the real box, not just weight.
On the diagram: ShipStation + labels, plus a consolidator replacing the EU partner.

G5One view of every location

  • Hanford and the Australian 3PL each become a location in Shopify with their own inventory. Order routing can send Australian orders to the Australian 3PL first.
  • China stock keeps moving in bulk to Hanford and Australia. That matters because the US has suspended duty-free de minimis treatment on low-value imports (U.S. CBP), so shipping single parcels from China to US players would add a duty step on every box.
On the diagram: the dashed green lines from Shopify to each location.

G6P500 works for international players

  • The P500 app keeps the card on file and charges when the game ships. Shopify's pre-order rules apply: customers can't use local payment methods for pre-orders, and supported gateways include Shopify Payments (shopify.dev).
  • Duties can change between the pre-order and the charge. GMT needs a business decision: absorb the change, or recalculate and pass it on when the order is charged.
On the diagram: the P500 pre-order app box, feeding the same duty and shipping flow at charge time.

Still to confirm

  • How Zonos shows duties at checkout (separate line or built into shipping), and whether it recalculates when a P500 order is charged later.
  • Whether DDP labels bought in ShipStation work with duties collected by Shopify at checkout. We'll test this before go-live.
  • Consolidator quotes on GMT's real box sizes into the EU, UK, Canada and Australia.
  • Whether orders shipped from the Australian 3PL are covered by the duty and tax engine, or handled as domestic Australian sales.
  • If GMT wants a merchant of record: whether that provider supports charge-later pre-orders and ship-from locations outside the US.
